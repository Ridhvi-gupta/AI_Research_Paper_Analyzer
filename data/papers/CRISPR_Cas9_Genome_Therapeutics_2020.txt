Targeted genome modification using engineered CRISPR-Cas9 ribonucleoproteins has transformed functional genomics and molecular therapeutics.
Single-guide RNA directed Cas9 endonucleases facilitate precise double-strand DNA cleavage at designated target loci across eukaryotic genomes.
However, our evaluation indicates that the methodology is constrained by off-target genomic cleavage and cytotoxic chromosomal translocations in human hematopoietic stem cells.
A persistent bottleneck remains in vivo delivery of editing vectors across the blood-brain barrier for treating neurodegenerative disorders.
Future work will focus on chemical modification of single-guide RNAs and high-fidelity Cas9 variants with reduced non-specific genomic interactions.
