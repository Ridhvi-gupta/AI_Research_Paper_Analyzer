Superconducting circuit architectures provide a modular and scalable pathway toward fault-tolerant quantum information processing.
We characterize a programmable 53-qubit quantum processor operating at dilution refrigerator temperatures near twenty millikelvin.
A severe limitation remains thermal decoherence and high crosstalk noise during simultaneous two-qubit gate pulses, which degrades two-qubit randomized benchmarking fidelities.
Furthermore, fault-tolerant surface code error correction necessitates thousands of physical qubits per logical qubit, a requirement that remains technologically unfeasible with current cryogenic dilution refrigeration payloads.
Future investigations must address 3D topological surface codes and high-impedance microwave resonators to extend quantum coherence lifetimes beyond five hundred microseconds.
